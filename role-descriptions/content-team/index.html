The big picture

Social media is not simply a tool to promote ministries; it itself can be a ministry.

We want to communicate to our church community and the broader community what is happening in the life of St Paul's. We want people to see the friendships, service, growth, fun and fellowship that are part of our church. We also want to share glimpses of the love and message of Jesus.

What you'll do

Capturing the life of St Paul's.

You'll capture photos and videos on your phone in the ministries you're already part of. You don't need to attend additional events or be a professional photographer. We simply want to build a bank of images/videos that helps us tell the story of our church. You capture — we edit and post.

01

Look for opportunities

Be intentional about noticing moments worth capturing in your ministry. This might be a group enjoying time together, people serving, a moment in a Sunday gathering or something happening during the week. There's no strict quota of how much to capture — try starting with one photo or video each week.

02

Capture photos and videos

Use your phone to take a few photos or short videos. Look for natural, genuine moments that communicate something about the life of our church. Quality is great, but capturing the moment matters most.

03

Upload your content

Upload your photos and videos to our shared Dropbox. You can upload raw footage, or add captions, text and simple designs if you'd like. There's no expectation to produce finished social media content. We'll take care of selecting, editing and publishing content.

04

Keep capturing

Make it an ongoing habit. You don't need to upload something every week, but whenever you capture something worth sharing, add it to the Dropbox so we can continue building our content library.

It doesn't need to be polished.

The Dropbox is a shared bank of content, not a submission of finished social media posts. A great candid photo or a 10-second video can be just as useful as something carefully edited. Don't let the desire to make something perfect stop you from capturing it!

Tips and guidance

If you need good sound...

If you're recording an interview, someone speaking or anything where sound matters, remember that your phone's microphone needs to be relatively close to the person speaking. Or—even better—if you give some advanced warning (even a couple hours' warning) we can lend you our church wireless microphone that hooks straight into your phone for capturing good audio.

A note about photographing people.

We want to respect people's privacy and make sure they're comfortable being photographed or filmed. Group photos are generally fine, but don't take close-up photos or videos of people without their express permission.

A few practical tips:

  • In social settings, ask naturally: "Hey, would you mind if I grabbed a quick photo of you guys for the church socials?"
  • Explain what the content is for. Let people know it may be shared on St Paul's social media.
  • Don't pressure anyone who would prefer not to be included. A simple "No worries at all!" is all that's needed.
  • Don't photograph or film children. If you see an opportunity to capture content involving children, speak to the ministry leader. Photography and filming of children should only be undertaken by authorised people, with the appropriate permissions in place.

When in doubt, ask first. We want people to feel valued and respected, not like they're being used for content.
