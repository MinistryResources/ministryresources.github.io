The big picture

A welcoming place to explore.

We want people to feel they can explore the claims of Christianity, whatever their background or level of understanding. By providing a regular context where non-believers can explore Christianity, we hope to see an increasing number of people make an informed decision to follow Jesus.

What does the Explore Team do?

The Explore Team runs two related ministries: Explore Christianity and Bible Explorers Groups.


Explore Christianity currently exists as a three-week course that covers the basics of Christianity. It runs once each term. We make it different each time so that people who have attended before can attend again (the gospel message stays the same; the Bible passages and illustrations change).


Bible Explorers Group is not a course, but a community that caters for people who want to keep exploring when Explore Christianity is not running. Currently we have a Bible Explorers Group that meets weekly on Tuesday nights during the school term, and one which meets weekly within our Wednesday Women ministry. In the future we'd love to provide more Bible Explorers Groups in a variety of timeslots for a variety of demographics. Members of Bible Explorers Groups are encouraged to attend Explore Christianity each time it runs.


Each Explore Christianity session and each Bible Explorers Group meeting are made up of a few ingredients: time exploring an aspect of Christian belief, guided discussion, and space to ask questions.

What you'll do

You'll be part of a team helping people explore Christianity through casual conversations and guided discussion. Your role is to help create a welcoming environment and encourage people to engage with the Bible and the big questions of life and faith.

01

Pray

Pray during the week for the people who come along, that God would help them see Jesus clearly.

02

Help with logistics

Arrive on time (7pm) on Tuesdays time to help set up. The team will often be done setting up 10-15mins before explorers arrive—but that's good. We want guests to turn up to a space where there are already people chatting.

03

Join the discussion

Take part in discussion time, helping people engage with Bible passages and think through what Christians believe.

04

Support one another

Help with practical tasks like setup, food, seating and pack down. Communicate with the team, share any pastoral concerns and take part in opportunities to reflect and pray together.

You don't need to have all the answers.

People will ask difficult questions, and sometimes you won't know what to say. That's okay! Be genuine, patient and non-defensive. We want to model a healthy habit of going back to the Bible rather than relying on our own opinions. Trust God to work in people's lives over time.

You don't need to join another Growth Group.

Being an Explore team member is similar to being a Growth Group leader in some ways. You are welcome to join another Growth Group as well if you would like to, but this is not a requirement. We don't pray together during our regular Explorer gatherings so that people who don't believe in prayer feel comfortable participating. To help us grow spiritually as a team, we'll meet separately at the end of each term to reflect on the Bible and pray together.

General tips

Helping people explore well.

A few things to keep in mind as you welcome people and join conversations. You don't need to be an expert evangelist. Often the most helpful thing you can do is listen well, ask a good question and help someone engage with the Bible.

01

Be proactive in saying hi

If you see someone standing by themselves or looking unsure, go and say hello. A friendly welcome can make a big difference.

02

Start conversations light

You might be itching to have a deep, evangelistic conversation with someone, but it's best to begin naturally. Ask how their day has been or what they do during the week.

03

Ask questions

Don't dominate discussion — try to get explorers speaking more than you. People generally like talking about themselves. Let them! Ask questions to get to know people and understand where they're coming from.

04

Don't make assumptions

Don't assume people are Christians or aren't Christians. There can be a mix of believers and non-believers in the room. Likewise, don't assume people know the basics of Christianity, the Bible or Christian language.

05

Encourage questions...

...even ones which challenge beliefs you hold dearly. Listen carefully and keep bringing the conversation back to Jesus. Thank people for being honest. Don't rush to shut questions down or tidy things up too quickly. We want people to feel free to explore difficult questions.

06

Keep pointing people to Jesus

When questions arise, point people back to Jesus and the Bible whenever you can. This models a healthy habit of going to God's revelation rather than our personal opinions about a topic.

07

Be real, not defensive

Sometimes it's helpful to share your own uncertainty about an issue or passage. Normal faith is more helpful than polished answers, which can sometimes come across as defensive.

08

Aim for clarity, not covering everything

You don't need to explain every issue in one night. It's better that people grasp one big idea than leave feeling confused.

09

Keep the main things the main things

Convey what Christians generally believe. Christians can hold different views on secondary topics like predestination, baptism and preaching. Don't be too rigid on issues where there is a breadth of opinion.

10

Be patient with where people are at

People process things at different speeds. Don't pressure responses or decisions. Trust God to work over time.

Growing in evangelism

Keep growing yourself.

The best way to grow in your evangelism is to give it a go and get real-life experience of it. But reading and reflecting is also really valuable! Here are some books worth reading if you'd like to develop further.

01

Mere Evangelism

Randy Newman

A thoughtful and practical guide to sharing faith through good questions and careful listening, drawing heavily on the writings and life of C.S. Lewis.

02

Evangelism

Mack Stiles

A clear, church-centred vision for evangelism that keeps the gospel front and centre. Great for grounding evangelism in ordinary Christian life rather than special events.

03

Nothing in My Hand I Bring

Ray Galea

A short, accessible book that explains the difference between Protestant and Roman Catholic beliefs. It explains grace and justification simply and pastorally.

04

The Reason for God

Tim Keller

Engages common intellectual objections to Christianity with clarity and empathy. Helpful for understanding how thoughtful non-Christians often think and where the gospel speaks into those questions.

05

Can We Trust What the Gospels Say About Jesus?

Andrew Errington

A straightforward introduction to the historical reliability of the Gospels. Useful for addressing doubts about whether the Jesus we meet in the Bible is trustworthy.

06

If I Were God, I'd End All the Pain

John Dickson

Tackles the problem of suffering in a clear, compassionate way. A great resource for conversations where pain and injustice are major barriers to belief.

07

God and Science

Scott Petty

Explores the relationship between Christian faith and modern science without being technical. Helpful for removing the assumption that faith and science are in conflict.
