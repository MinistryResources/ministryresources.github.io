What you'll do

Capturing the life of St Paul's.

You'll take responsibility for capturing occasional photos and videos on your phone in the ministries you're already part of. You don't need to attend additional events or be a professional photographer. We simply want to build a bank of genuine content that helps tell the story of our church.

01

Look for opportunities

Be intentional about noticing moments worth capturing in your ministry. This might be a group enjoying time together, people serving, a great moment in a Sunday gathering or something happening during the week.

02

Capture photos and videos

Use your phone to take a few photos or short videos. Look for natural, genuine moments that communicate something about the life of our church. Quality is great, but capturing the moment matters most.

03

Upload your content

Upload your photos and videos to our shared Dropbox. You can upload raw footage, or add captions, text and simple designs if you'd like. There's no expectation to produce finished social media content. We'll take care of selecting, editing and publishing content.

04

Keep capturing

Make it an ongoing habit. You don't need to upload something every week, but whenever you capture something worth sharing, add it to the Dropbox so we can continue building our content library.

It doesn't need to be polished.

The Dropbox is a shared bank of content, not a submission of finished social media posts. A great candid photo or a 10-second video can be just as useful as something carefully edited. Don't let the desire to make something perfect stop you from capturing and sharing it!

Tips and guidance

Getting good content.

A few simple things can make a big difference to the quality and usefulness of the content you capture. Most importantly, keep it natural and remember that we're capturing real life, not staging a photoshoot.

01

Capture genuine moments

Look for people interacting, laughing, serving, singing, listening and enjoying being together. Candid moments often tell a better story than posed photos. Try to capture a variety of people, activities and settings.

02

Think about your framing

Take a few different shots. Include some wider photos that show the environment and group, as well as closer shots of activities and details. Keep your phone steady, make sure there's enough light and avoid zooming in too much.

03

Take videos in 4K

Video is particularly useful for social media. If you're using an iPhone, set your camera to record in 4K (Settings → Camera → Record Video → 4K). Capture short clips, hold your phone steady and try to avoid unnecessary camera movement. Vertical video is particularly useful for Instagram Reels and Stories.

04

Get close enough for sound

If you're recording an interview, someone speaking or anything where sound matters, remember that your phone's microphone needs to be relatively close to the person speaking. If you want to capture something from the front of a room, speak to the ministry leader beforehand about accessing a microphone or audio feed.

05

Don't worry about editing

You're welcome to add captions, text, graphics or simple designs to your content, but you don't have to. Raw photos and videos are often exactly what we need. Just upload what you've captured.

A note about photographing people.

We want to respect people's privacy and make sure they're comfortable being photographed or filmed.

Group photos are generally fine, but don't take close-up photos or videos of identifiable individuals without their express permission.

A few practical tips:

  • In social settings, ask naturally: "Hey, would you mind if I grabbed a quick photo of you guys for the church socials?"
  • Explain what the content is for. Let people know it may be shared on St Paul's social media.
  • Ask before taking a close-up, rather than assuming someone is comfortable being photographed.
  • Don't pressure anyone who would prefer not to be included. A simple "No worries at all!" is all that's needed.
  • Be particularly careful when photographing children. Follow St Paul's Safe Ministry and photography requirements, and check with the relevant ministry leader before taking or uploading identifiable images of children.

When in doubt, ask first. We want people to feel valued and respected, not like they're being used for content.
